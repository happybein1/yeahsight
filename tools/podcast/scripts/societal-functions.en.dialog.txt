E: Three?
S: Social cohesion. Enough trust and shared identity for people to act together. When trust breaks down, every agreement needs checking and every disagreement risks becoming a fight. A society can split into hostile camps, or simply into people who no longer care about each other.

S: Passing on knowledge. Handing skills and values to the next generation, through schools, but also families, workplaces and communities. Otherwise each generation would start from zero. It works when people can read, count, think critically and keep learning.
E: Five?
S: Care. Looking after those who can't fully look after themselves: children, older people, the sick, people with disabilities. Everyone needs care at the start of life, often at the end, and sometimes in between. How a society treats its most vulnerable is a direct test of whether it serves everyone.
E: Six?
S: Settling disputes. Disagreement is normal in any group. What matters is that it's settled by rules and discussion, not force. You see it working when laws apply equally to everyone, ordinary people can get justice, and governments change peacefully.

S: Adapting to change. New technologies, a changing environment, an ageing population. The conditions a society depends on never stay the same, and one that can't adapt eventually fails at all the other tasks.
