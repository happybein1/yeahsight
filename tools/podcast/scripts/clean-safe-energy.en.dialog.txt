S: Turbines turn moving air into electricity. Wind on land is among the cheapest electricity there is. Wind at sea costs more, but blows stronger and more steadily. The challenges: it also comes and goes, some people dislike how turbines look and sound, and they have to be placed with care for birds and bats.
E: Hydropower?
S: Electricity from moving water, and the largest source of renewable electricity in the world. It's reliable and can be turned up or down, which helps keep the grid stable. But big dams have serious effects on nature and on the people living nearby, and in many regions the good sites are already used.
E: And nuclear. Always a debate.
S: The article lists it as clean in the sense of emissions: over its whole life, nuclear produces very little greenhouse gas, and it gives steady, controllable power. The challenges are real too: high building costs, long construction times, managing the waste, and public trust.
E: So no perfect source.
S: No. Each has trade-offs, which is why most plans combine several, plus storage and stronger grids.
E: What can one person do?
S: Switch to a renewable electricity contract if one is available. Think about solar panels if you own your home. Use less energy: insulation, efficient appliances, small habits. And choose an electric car over a petrol or diesel one where that's possible.
